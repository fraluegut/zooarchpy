# References

Primary sources must be consulted before implementing algorithms derived from them.

**Status legend.** Each entry is marked `[read]` once a maintainer has consulted the primary
source and checked that the algorithm in the code matches it, or `[to verify]` if it is cited
from memory or secondary literature and still needs to be read. Nothing marked `[to verify]`
may be implemented as a method until it is read.

## Fragment-based MNE and MNI (planned, Phase 2)

Background for deciding whether two fragments can belong to the same bone
(shared diagnostic zone ⇒ different elements; see the design notes for MNE/MNI).

- White, T. E. (1953). A method of calculating the dietary percentage of various food animals utilized by aboriginal peoples. *American Antiquity*, 18(4), 396–398. Origin of MNI. `[to verify]`
- Watson, J. P. N. (1979). The estimation of the relative frequencies of mammalian species: Khirokitia 1972. *Journal of Archaeological Science*, 6, 127–137. Zone-based counting. `[to verify]`
- Klein, R. G. & Cruz-Uribe, K. (1984). *The Analysis of Animal Bones from Archaeological Sites*. University of Chicago Press. MNE/MNI from fragments. `[to verify]`
- Dobney, K. & Rielly, K. (1988). A method for recording archaeological animal bones: the use of diagnostic zones. *Circaea*, 5(2), 79–96. Diagnostic zones per element. `[to verify]`
- Marean, C. W. & Spencer, L. M. (1991). Impact of carnivore ravaging on zooarchaeological measures of element abundance. *American Antiquity*, 56(4), 645–658. Fragmentation and element representation. `[to verify]`

Zone schemes for specific taxonomic groups (mammals, birds, fish...) will be listed here
with the exact source of each scheme when they are added; the library does not invent them.

Per-method references are listed in each method's page (e.g. [nisp.md](nisp.md)).
